Fieldcraft pocket reference cards

Fieldcraft v1.0 beta · Educational reference, not professional training. In immediate danger call emergency services. Check current official guidance.

Serious injury / illness

  1. Check that the scene is safe before approaching.
  2. Call 911 for life-threatening conditions; ask someone to bring an AED if available.
  3. Follow the dispatcher and give care within your training. If unresponsive and not breathing normally, start CPR and use an AED as directed.
Source: American Red Cross · https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/first-aid/performing-first-aid/first-aid-steps

Lost outdoors

  1. Stop moving unless remaining there is dangerous.
  2. Contact emergency services or use an emergency communicator if available. Report your last known position, injuries and group size.
  3. Stay visible, shelter from the weather, conserve battery power and signal for help.
Source: National Park Service · https://www.nps.gov/grte/planyourvisit/safety.htm

Lightning

  1. When thunder is heard, move into a substantial enclosed building or hard-topped enclosed vehicle.
  2. A tent, picnic shelter or isolated tree is not safe lightning shelter.
  3. Wait at least 30 minutes after the last thunder before going back outside.
Source: National Weather Service · https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-tips

Flooding

  1. Leave low-lying locations promptly and move to higher ground.
  2. Never walk or drive into floodwater; turn around at inundated roads.
  3. Follow evacuation orders; call for help if trapped.
Source: National Weather Service · https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood-during

Unsafe drinking water

  1. Follow local water advisories; use bottled or otherwise confirmed safe water when possible.
  2. For germ contamination, bring clear water to a rolling boil for 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 6,500 ft (about 2,000 m).
  3. Cool and store in clean covered containers. Boiling does not remove fuel, chemicals or radioactive contamination; use another source.
Source: CDC · https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/

Power outage / generator

  1. Operate fuel-burning generators outside, at least 20 feet from buildings, with exhaust pointing away from openings.
  2. Never operate a generator or charcoal grill inside a home, garage or tent.
  3. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds or poisoning is suspected, move to fresh air and call 911.
Source: CPSC · https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Carbon-Monoxide-Information-Center

Wildfire nearby

  1. Monitor official alerts if available and leave immediately when ordered.
  2. Use official evacuation routes; do not travel into smoke or toward the fire.
  3. Do not attempt firefighting or return until officials say it is safe.
Source: Ready.gov · https://www.ready.gov/wildfires

Emergency signaling

  1. Use working emergency communications first when possible.
  2. Use a whistle, light or visible marker to attract rescuers without exposing yourself to hazards.
  3. SOS is three short, three long, three short signals. An app-generated tone alone does not contact rescue.
Source: ITU / NPS · https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-M.1677/en
